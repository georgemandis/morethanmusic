## The idea

The talk was a sandwich. Ten minutes of what MIDI is, ten minutes of what a message looks like in
JavaScript, and then demos until the time ran out. The 2017 taskpaper file lists the first batch of
ideas as "Web-based instrument, Color Mixer, Quiz interface, Kiosk/Menu/Selector, Escape Room
Interface, Goonies Organ Scene, Combining with Web Sockets, Video Player Interface, 2-Way Interaction
with a DrumPad". The demos folder is what survived contact with a stage.

They were served from one folder with `http-server .` so they could share a `js` and `css` folder,
and the folder's `index.html` is a grid of grey tiles that go green when you hover. On the day it
was the launcher.

## A few of them, in the words of the notes

The Websocket Symphony opened every 2017 talk. From the HolyJS notes: "Connect to a wifi network
called 'Sputnik.' That's being generated by this little guy here. I've never tested this with more
than... 10." Everyone's phone became a note. Symphony II did the same with a MIDI file, spreading
the tracks across the room. The JSFoo outline: "I play parts of the MIDI file on different computers
out among the crowd."

The Hot Hand is a ring with three accelerometers that Source Audio sells to guitarists. The notes
call it "a crappy version of minority report" and say the obvious application was a theremin, "but
I wanted to recognize gestures." It became the Motion Color Mixer and the Motion Color Game.

The Goonies Organ came from an idea about escape rooms. The notes: "Had an idea to build escape room
components. Kind of over-promised and couldn't follow through but still think that's a solid idea."
The folder has a skull, a treasure, a photo of the skeleton organ and
a sheet of music drawn with VexFlow, and the HolyJS notes file it under "Story: escape rooms
application."

The Controller Explorer is the one that lasted. The taskpaper has the whole spec: "Make a MIDI device
exploration frontend tool. Select input. Drop down. Show numbers type of event and numbers. That's
it. Not rocket science." Nine years later it is the Explorer on this site.

The deck itself was a demo. A Puck.js in my hand sent a control change over Bluetooth MIDI for a
single, double or triple click, and a foot controller did the rest. `slide-deck-breakdown.html` in
the talk folder is a mini deck about that, with the advice to find controllers by name rather than by
their position in `WebMidi.inputs`, "so I can read this later and have a semblance of remembering
what controllers I was using." The clicker's code is public at
[puck-js-midi-clicker](https://github.com/georgemandis/puck-js-midi-clicker).
